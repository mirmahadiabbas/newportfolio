UK Visa Sponsor Licence SearchLive

Search the Home Office register of licensed sponsors by company name, visa route, or location. Built after needing this check repeatedly during a UK job search, where the official register is published as a large spreadsheet that is awkward to search.

Read this first

This tool searches the public UK Home Office register of licensed sponsors. It is not immigration advice, and it is not affiliated with the Home Office or UK Visas and Immigration.

The data shown is a snapshot of the register as published on 31 July 2026 and is not updated automatically. Licence status changes frequently: a company listed here may no longer hold a licence, and a company added since that date will not appear.

A company not appearing in these results does not mean it cannot sponsor a visa. It may be registered under a different legal name to the one it trades under, may have joined the register after this snapshot, or may be spelled differently in the register.

Holding a sponsor licence does not mean a company is willing to sponsor any particular role or candidate. Always confirm current status against the official register on GOV.UK and check directly with the employer.

Official source: gov.uk/government/publications/register-of-licensed-sponsors-workers

The decision worth explaining: no AI in this one

Searching a fixed reference dataset is a data problem, not a language problem. A model would have been slower, costlier and less accurate than a plain string scan, and it could have invented a company that is not on the register. Choosing not to use AI was the design decision here.

Other decisions

  • The architecture was chosen against the platform's billing model. A SQL database was the obvious first choice, but its free tier charges reads by rows scanned and caps daily writes, which suits incremental data rather than a read-only reference file of this size. A static asset searched in memory fits the workload and stays free.
  • Around 142,000 register entries, searched server-side, with results capped and paginated so no single query can do unbounded work.
  • The dataset is kept exactly as published: companies licensed for several routes appear once per route, matching the source rather than reshaping it.
